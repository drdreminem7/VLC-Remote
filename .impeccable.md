# Design context

## Target audience

A single Mac owner using their phone at home, often in a dim room while media
is playing on a larger screen. They need controls to be legible at a glance and
easy to hit without giving the phone their full attention.

## Primary use cases

- Pair a phone securely with the Mac-hosted remote.
- Confirm that the Mac service and VLC are reachable.
- Read the current title and playback time.
- Operate essential playback controls quickly with one hand.
- Recover confidently when Wi-Fi, the backend, or VLC becomes unavailable.
- Install the remote as a home-screen PWA.

## Brand personality

Quiet, cinematic, and tactile. The interface should feel like a purpose-built
physical remote translated to a phone: low-light, restrained, confident, and
highly legible. It should avoid both neon “gamer” styling and generic SaaS
dashboard patterns.

## Phase 1 visual direction

- Use a dark, warm-neutral palette suited to low-light viewing.
- Reserve a warm amber accent for the primary action and active state.
- Prefer strong typographic hierarchy, large touch targets, and a small number
  of layered surfaces.
- Use asymmetry and spacing to separate status, media, and controls.
- Keep motion subtle and state-driven, respecting reduced-motion preferences.

## Phase 2 remote-surface brief

### Feature summary

The interface is a phone-first controller for one person operating VLC from a
sofa or bed. It must make connection state and essential playback controls
legible at a glance while the television remains the visual focus.

### Primary user action

Recognize the current playback state and press the central play/pause control
without hunting through the interface.

### Design direction

Treat the application as a quiet physical object rather than a dashboard:
monochrome, sculpted, tactile, and cinematic. Draw from the supplied remote
reference's large textured touch surface, circular controls, sparse labels, and
paired light/dark materials without copying its branding or exact layout.

### Layout strategy

- Place room and connection context in a compact top rail.
- Give the central touch surface most of the vertical space; use it for media
  identity, time, timeline, and honest unavailable/empty messaging.
- Keep transport controls in the lower thumb zone with play/pause dominant.
- On wider screens, frame the remote as a physical object and use the spare
  space for a short product caption rather than stretching the controls.

### Key states

- Backend ready while the live VLC adapter remains safely unconfigured.
- Connecting, paired, playing, paused, VLC unavailable, unauthenticated, and
  phone offline states in later phases.
- Disabled controls remain clearly legible and explain why they are inactive.

### Interaction model

All controls use semantic buttons, visible focus, at least 44px targets, and a
short pressed-in response. Hover is enhancement only. Motion communicates
state changes and is removed when reduced motion is requested.

### Content requirements

Use compact, direct labels: “Living room Mac”, “Backend ready”, “VLC setup
paused”, and “Live control stays off until VLC can run safely.” Never suggest
re-enabling the known-broken Web/Lua configuration.

### Recommended references

Use the impeccable spatial, typography, color/contrast, interaction, motion,
responsive, and UX-writing references together with the adapt skill.

### Open questions

None for this checkpoint. OS color preference controls light/dark appearance;
manual theme selection can be considered with the complete Phase 3 interface.
